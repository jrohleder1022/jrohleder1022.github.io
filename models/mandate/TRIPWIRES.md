# Standing tripwires

Written exit conditions for every position. §7 of the policy sells **falling
value, never falling price** — so none of these is a price level. Each is a
business fact observable from a filing, a release, or a regulatory action,
independent of the quote.

A tripwire exists so the decision is made now, in calm, rather than later,
in the sixty seconds after a headline. It worked once already: the NVIDIA
condition fired on 12 November 2026 and was executed without debate.

---

## SPCX — bought 13 Nov 2026, $1,500, core

**Thesis as stated by the owner:** sovereign-scale launch infrastructure.
The only entity with heavy-lift at cadence, the only US crew transport, the
Artemis lander, the only operating LEO broadband constellation at scale. No
substitute and no credible path to one inside a decade.

> **SPCX-1 — thesis drift. The one that matters most.**
> "Super Intelligence" revenue was **52% of Q3 2026 revenue**, outselling
> Starlink for the first time. **If AI exceeds 65% of revenue, the company
> owned is no longer the company bought.** The thesis above is space
> irreplaceability; at 65%+ the majority of the business is something that is
> not irreplaceable at all, and the franchise premium does not apply to it.
> This tests the actual reason for ownership rather than execution.

> **SPCX-2 — capex discipline.**
> Q4 2026 guided to **$35–40B against $14.6B quarterly revenue (~250%)**.
> Defensible while revenue compounds at 161%. **If capex stays above 200% of
> revenue for four consecutive quarters while revenue growth falls below
> 50%**, the company is funding capacity it cannot monetize.

> **SPCX-3 — the franchise itself.**
> Starlink ARPU is **$69** — up from $66 sequentially, **down from $83 a year
> earlier**. Falling per-user economics is what commoditization looks like.
> **Two further quarters of year-over-year ARPU decline** means the broadband
> business is a price-taker rather than a monopoly.

## SNPS — core

> **SNPS-1 — policy.** A BIS action that *tightens* the EDA regime beyond
> today's license requirement: license-required moving to license-denied, or
> an expansion of restricted ECCNs. **The action itself, not a headline about
> proposed legislation.**

> **SNPS-2 — the real one.** Aggregate R&D spending at the top five
> semiconductor customers declines year over year. EDA revenue is a take-rate
> on chip R&D budgets; if the budgets shrink, the business shrinks regardless
> of share or software quality. **China is a dent; an R&D recession is the
> thesis breaking.**

> **SNPS-3 — the Ansys question.** Blended ROIC *including goodwill* should
> **rise year over year** as acquired earnings scale into a now-fixed capital
> base. Ex-goodwill ROIC is ~27% against an ~8.2% WACC, so the operations are
> excellent; the blended figure is ~7.7%, so the $35B price is not yet earning
> its cost. **Flat or falling by FY2028** means the growth was bought rather
> than generated.

## NOW — core

> **NOW-1 — cRPO deceleration.** **Two consecutive quarters of decelerating
> cRPO growth.** cRPO leads revenue by roughly four quarters, so it is the
> only metric that shows seat compression before it reaches the income
> statement. Q4 2026 guidance already implies one quarter of deceleration;
> **the Q4 print is the test.**
>
> *Two earlier versions of this tripwire were written on gross margin and then
> FCF margin. Both were lagging measures and both missed — FCF margin guidance
> was raised to 36% while the stock fell 44%. Profitability metrics describe a
> quarter that has already closed.*

## GOOGL — core

> **GOOGL-1 — structural remedy.** A court-ordered divestiture of Chrome,
> Android, or the ad-tech stack. The September 2025 remedies ruling avoided
> this and capped the tail; a reversal on appeal reopens it.

> **GOOGL-2 — search query decline.** Core search query volume declining year
> over year for two consecutive quarters. Advertising is still effectively all
> of near-term operating profit; query volume is the leading indicator for it.

## NVDA — sold 12 Nov 2026. Re-entry conditions, all three required.

1. **A permanent CEO** who can articulate an architectural thesis for 2029
   and holds credibility with TSMC and Washington. An operator who can run the
   factory is sufficient for an Apple; it is not sufficient for a company
   whose five largest customers are building its replacement.
2. **Data center revenue growth holding above ~20%** post-transition.
3. **The policy picture resolved enough to underwrite:** the NDAA cycle
   closed so the Chip Security Act and AI Overwatch Act are readable as
   enacted law, and the congressional investigations producing findings rather
   than headlines.

*Not a trigger: a lower price. Cheap is not a thesis.*

## Candidates — conditions that would make them investable

**RKLB** — first successful Neutron flight **and** a narrowing GAAP loss.
Currently a $79.7M quarterly loss that quadrupled year over year, with the
medium-lift thesis gated on a 2027 first flight.

**CBRS** — three of four: largest customer below 20% of revenue; positive
operating income; a hyperscaler standardizing rather than piloting;
unprompted third-party developer adoption.

**MU** — not investable on a low P/E. Gross margin is 87% against a 25–35%
mid-cycle mean, so the earnings in the denominator are a peak. Value it off
mid-cycle earnings or not at all.

**CRWV** — evidence that GPU economic life matches the assumed depreciation
schedule, and interest expense falling below 15% of revenue. Currently 23%
and guided higher.
